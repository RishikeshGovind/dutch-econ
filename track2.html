Staffing the Ward Under Forecast Error
Cost and Service Risk

This page applies the same predict-then-optimize idea to a hospital setting. A teaching hospital forecasts ICU admissions for the next day and uses that forecast to plan nurse staffing. The catch is that being short-staffed costs far more than having too many staff on shift.

The asymmetric loss problem Standard XGBoost treats overpredicting and underpredicting patient numbers as equally bad. In practice, calling in extra staff on a quiet day costs much less than being short-staffed on a busy one. SPO+ would train the forecast to care about that difference directly.

The staffing horizon problem ICU shifts are usually set 12 to 24 hours in advance. Monday morning admissions can swing by around 40% depending on how many patients were discharged over the weekend. A model trained on staffing cost rather than pure accuracy should handle this kind of pattern better.

Asymmetry in action Overstaffing adds a slow steady cost day by day. Short-staffed shifts are rare but expensive and show up as sharp jumps in the cost line. In this prototype, just a few missed surges account for most of the total gap.

Why SPO+ fits hospitals Standard XGBoost ignores the cost difference: a short-staffed shift costs around €180 per patient-hour versus €45 for staff sitting idle. SPO+ (Elmachtoub & Grigas, 2022) trains the model to reduce the actual staffing cost, not just the prediction error, focusing on the surge windows shown in the clock above.

Feature Engineering & Importances

XGBoost trained on real RIVM national ICU admission data (Oct 2021–Apr 2024), scaled to a single teaching hospital (40 IC beds, 3.5% of national capacity) and combined with a NICE-calibrated non-COVID occupancy baseline. The model predicts tomorrow's total ICU occupancy — the number the LP uses to set staffing. Importance = mean gain across all trees.

18 features  ·  1 row = 1 day  ·  716 training samples (2022–2023)
