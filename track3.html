The Last-Mile Problem:
When Routing Meets Forecasting

This page applies the same research question to parcel delivery. A Dutch carrier forecasts daily parcel demand by postcode zone and uses that forecast to plan delivery routes using a Capacitated VRP. Routing at this scale is too complex to solve exactly, so heuristics are used instead. That is what makes this case different from the energy and hospital tracks.

Why VRP motivates SPO+ There is no provably optimal route at this scale, so the green benchmark is just the best found, not a guarantee. SPO+ trained on route cost would learn to focus on zones where a bad demand forecast adds the most distance, not just the zones where the number is furthest off. Track 1 and Track 2 both use a linear program you can differentiate through. This track cannot.
Data & Methods Demand: synthetic parcel volumes calibrated on CBS Kerncijfers wijken en buurten (postcode-level demographics, household density) · Road network: OpenStreetMap Netherlands · Vehicles: RDW registered fleet data · 12 Amsterdam postcode zones (approximate centroids on the Amsterdam street map) · Routing: capacitated VRP · nearest-neighbour + 2-opt · Demand forecast: XGBoost on CBS parcel proxy features

The research gap this track opens Track 1 uses a linear program you can differentiate through. Track 2 adds asymmetric costs to that LP. This track replaces the LP with a heuristic solver that cannot be differentiated directly. Extending SPO+ to work here, using things like perturbation-based gradients or surrogate losses, is the open question that connects all three tracks.
