Track 3 · Predict-Then-Optimize

The Last-Mile Problem:
When Routing Meets Forecasting

A Dutch last-mile carrier predicts daily parcel demand per postcode zone from CBS demographic data and day-of-week patterns, then solves a Capacitated VRP. The routing problem is NP-hard — exact solutions are intractable at scale — making this the natural test bed for heuristic-enhanced predict-then-optimize.

Why VRP motivates SPO+ With NP-hard routing, no exact oracle exists at scale — the "oracle" is itself a heuristic upper bound. SPO+ trained on route cost regret learns to forecast demand where prediction error most inflates route distance, not where MSE is largest. This is the methodological gap that Track 1 (LP) and Track 2 (asymmetric LP) do not reach.

Demand variability drives route variance A ±20% demand error in a high-density zone can force a vehicle to make an unplanned second sweep, adding 8–12 km per vehicle per day. The VRP solver needs accurate demand before it can produce good routes.

The research gap this track opens Track 1 optimises a continuous LP. Track 2 introduces asymmetric loss into the LP. Track 3 replaces the LP with an NP-hard heuristic — the solver is no longer differentiable. SPO+ must be adapted for combinatorial settings (perturbation-based gradients, surrogate losses), which is the open methodological frontier connecting energy, hospitals, and logistics.
