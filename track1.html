Track 1 · Predict-Then-Optimize

Between Oracle and Reality:
A 60-Day Energy Storage Experiment

A 1 MW / 2 MWh battery buys electricity from the Dutch day-ahead market when prices are cheap and sells it back when prices spike. The catch is that prices have to be predicted 24 hours before you can act on them. This project tracks what that forecast error actually costs.

The research question Getting the forecast as accurate as possible is not the same as making better charging decisions. The charts below show where the gap comes from and why a different training approach (SPO+) could close it.
Data & Methods Price data: ENTSO-E Transparency Platform NL day-ahead prices (bidding zone NL) · Jan 2021 to Sep 2024 · 34,320 hourly observations · Forecast model: XGBoost · 24 engineered features · trained on 3-year history · Optimiser: linear program (LP) · 1 MW / 2 MWh battery · round-trip efficiency 90% · Test period: Oct to Nov 2024 · 60 out-of-sample days

What the Market Looks Like

Dutch day-ahead prices across 14 test days and 24 hours. Purple means negative prices, when there is too much renewable power on the grid. Deep orange is where prices spike. The battery needs to read these patterns from a forecast made the day before.

⚡
Why negative prices matter When prices go negative, the grid pays you to consume. The battery can charge for free and sell later at a peak. The whole job of the forecast is to spot these windows 24 hours in advance.

Where the gap comes from The gap grows fastest on days when prices were volatile and the forecast missed a big move. SPO+ targets these days specifically, because it trains on the cost of wrong decisions rather than just how far off the number was.

When Forecast Errors Matter

Not all forecast errors matter the same. A 10 €/MWh miss at 3 am makes no difference to what the battery does. The same error at 6 pm can flip the charge or discharge decision completely.

Why SPO+ helps here Standard XGBoost tries to be accurate across all 24 hours equally. Smart Predict-Then-Optimize (Elmachtoub & Grigas, 2022) trains the model to focus on getting the hours right where errors actually change decisions, as shown in the clock above.
