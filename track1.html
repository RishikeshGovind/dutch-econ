Between Oracle and Reality:
A 60-Day Energy Storage Experiment

A 1 MW / 2 MWh battery charges from the Dutch day-ahead electricity market when prices are low and sells when they peak. The only problem — prices must be forecast 24 hours ahead. How much does forecast error cost?

The Research Question Minimising forecast MSE is not the same as maximising dispatch profit. The sections below trace exactly where and why this gap opens — and motivate decision-focused learning (SPO+) as the remedy.

What the Market Looks Like

Why this matters for the LP Negative price hours are golden — the grid pays you to consume. The battery's entire profit depends on forecasting these relative rankings 24 hours early.

The Growing Gap

Cumulative revenue over 60 test days. The oracle (dashed) is the upper bound of what is physically achievable with perfect foresight. Every missed negative-price hour and misjudged peak compounds the loss.

📉
The compounding effect The gap spikes on high-volatility days where price events were missed or misforecast. These are exactly the days SPO+ is designed to target by training on decision regret, not prediction error.

When Forecast Errors Matter

Not all forecast errors are equal. A 10 €/MWh error at 3 am changes nothing; the same error at 6 pm can flip the charge/discharge decision entirely.
